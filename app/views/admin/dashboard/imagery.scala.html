Imagery #

Street-view imagery gets re-driven, and an audit performed on imagery that has since been replaced may describe a curb ramp, obstacle, or crosswalk that no longer exists. Each night a rotating batch of streets is polled for current capture dates, audits older than that imagery are flagged, and street priority is recalculated so Explore sends labelers back down those streets — after unaudited ones. This page shows both halves of that: the ranking labelers are actually routed on, and whether the nightly jobs behind it are still running.

Re-audit frontier #

Every street Explore can currently route to, colored by its priority tier. Hover a segment for its priority and the audit counts behind it; click to pin its region here and in the tables below.

The four tiers are not four separate things to track — they are one ordering. Every street carries a single priority number, and higher is served sooner:

priority = 1 / (1 + current audits + ½ if any audit is on replaced imagery + ¼ per low-quality audit)

So the tiers read top to bottom as how much current-imagery coverage a street has: none ever, none anymore, one, several.

Region ranking #

Explore picks a region before it picks a street, so the region is the unit that decides where labelers get sent, and this is that ranking. It takes the five regions with the highest mean priority and chooses one of them at random, then a street within it — again at random among that region's top-priority ties. The four tier columns count streets and add up to Streets. Hover or click a row to brush its streets on the map above.

Next streets #

The highest-priority streets, with the audit counts that produced each value. Pin a region above to narrow this to that region's queue.

Is the pipeline alive? #

A re-audit flag can only come from the nightly imagery-age poll, so a poll that stops — an expired API key, a rate limit, an unsupported provider — looks exactly like a city whose imagery never changes. These are the jobs' own recorded runs: if the poll stopped firing, or fired and polled nothing, it shows here rather than only in the server log.

Streets polled per night

The dashed line is the nightly batch size — the number of streets the poll asks for — so polled plus skipped should reach it on a healthy night, or fall short only when fewer streets are eligible. Polled means the street answered conclusively and had its imagery dates written, advancing the rotation. Skipped means at least one of its three sample points came back inconclusive — an expired or rotated API key, a timeout, an unexpected response — so the street is left un-bumped and retried the next night. A confirmed "no imagery here" is not a skip; that is a conclusive answer. On a dev machine the dummy API keys make every call inconclusive, so everything is skipped by design.

Audits flagged and unflagged per night

Flags are raised when at least half a street's sampled points show imagery newer than the audit, and cleared when a later poll walks that median back. Both directions are normal; a long flat run of zeros while the poll is covering ground is not.

Rotation and freshness #

The poll works through a fixed number of streets a night, audited streets first, so it is a rotation rather than a census: how long it takes to come back around bounds how stale a re-audit flag can be. A street with no capture date polled yet is unmeasured, not up to date — it is counted separately below for exactly that reason.

How stale our labels are

Only the streets whose polled imagery is newer than their most recent audit — Google has already re-driven these, so the labels on them describe a curb ramp, obstacle, or crosswalk that may no longer be there. Every bar is work waiting on a labeler, and further right means the labels have been wrong for longer. Nothing here says where Google still needs to drive; that is not something we can act on. Streets whose audits still match their imagery are not plotted; they are the "audits still current" row in the rotation table above.
