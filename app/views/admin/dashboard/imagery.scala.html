Imagery #

Street-view imagery gets re-driven, and an audit performed on imagery that has since been replaced may describe a curb ramp, obstacle, or crosswalk that no longer exists. Each night a rotating batch of streets is polled for current capture dates, audits older than that imagery are flagged, and street priority is recalculated so Explore sends labelers back down those streets — after unaudited ones. This page shows both halves of that: the ranking labelers are actually routed on, and whether the nightly jobs behind it are still running.

Re-audit frontier #

Every street Explore can currently route to, colored by its priority tier. Priority is a single number per street — higher means served sooner — and the tiers are the shape that number actually takes: a street nobody has audited outranks one whose only audits are on replaced imagery, which in turn outranks a freshly audited street. Streets needing a re-audit are drawn dashed, matching the Label Map. Hover a segment for its priority and the audit counts behind it; click to pin its region here and in the tables below.

Where the work is #

Explore picks a region first — among those with the highest mean priority — and only then a street within it, at random among that region's top-priority ties. So the region is the unit that decides where labelers get sent, and this is that ranking. Hover or click a row to brush its streets on the map above.

Next streets #

The highest-priority streets, with the audit counts that produced each value. Pin a region above to narrow this to that region's queue.

Is the pipeline alive? #

A re-audit flag can only come from the nightly imagery-age poll, so a poll that stops — an expired API key, a rate limit, an unsupported provider — looks exactly like a city whose imagery never changes. These are the jobs' own recorded runs: if the poll stopped firing, or fired and polled nothing, it shows here rather than only in the server log.

Streets polled per night

Polled streets are the ones that answered conclusively and had their imagery dates refreshed; skipped ones gave an inconclusive answer and are retried the next night. A night of zeros with no failure means the poll ran and found nothing to do — check the batch size and the rotation below.

Audits flagged and unflagged per night

Flags are raised when at least half a street's sampled points show imagery newer than the audit, and cleared when a later poll walks that median back. Both directions are normal; a long flat run of zeros while the poll is covering ground is not.

Rotation and freshness #

The poll works through a fixed number of streets a night, audited streets first, so it is a rotation rather than a census: how long it takes to come back around bounds how stale a re-audit flag can be. A street with no capture date polled yet is unmeasured, not up to date — it is counted separately below for exactly that reason.

How much newer the imagery is

For streets that have been both audited and polled: the gap between the polled median capture date and the street's most recent audit. Everything to the right of zero is imagery the city has not been labeled on.
