Seattle · your city

Where it all began

Seattle was Project Sidewalk’s first city. Its volunteers have mapped 3,400+ miles and trained the AI models now helping every other deployment.

How Seattle mapped itself →
Newberg, OR

$50,000 for immediate repairs

After volunteers mapped the city, Newberg’s Council created a sidewalk renovation program — including grants that help homeowners fix the sidewalk outside their door.

Burnaby, BC

Built into the city’s plan

Project Sidewalk data feeds Burnaby’s New Sidewalks Program prioritization and its adopted Accessibility Strategy — shaped in forums with seniors, disabled residents, and city staff.

Mendota, IL

A safer walk to school

Community partners used crowdsourced data to win a Safe Routes to School grant, improving the paths kids take every morning.

Chicago, IL

Ward budgets, guided by residents

Four aldermanic wards used residents’ data to prioritize sidewalk spending equitably — and the Pedestrian Safety Committee heard expert testimony for moving beyond complaint-driven repairs.

Lincolnwood & Roselle, IL

ADA plans, powered by volunteers

Crowdsourced labels, combined with field checks, became formal ADA Transition Plans — the documents that will reshape both cities’ accessibility.

Zürich

An official open-data source

Zürich publishes Project Sidewalk data on the city’s open-data portal — work that won the Smart City Hub Switzerland Award for citizen science.

Oradell, NJ

Scouts changed their town’s mind

Girl Scouts mapped Oradell, presented findings to the City Council, and measurably shifted attitudes about disability and mobility — theirs and their neighbors’.

Chicagoland

Ten community groups mobilized

A stipend program with the Active Transportation Alliance equipped ten local groups to map conditions, brief officials, and back grant applications.
